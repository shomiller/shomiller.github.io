This is an informal reading course on supersymmetry, intended for advanced graduate students in particle theory. We'll assume a background in quantum field theory and the Standard Model at the level of the graduate course sequences here at Pitt, though we'll try to make the course as self-contained as possible. Lecture notes and homeworks will be provided to help internalize the material, though there is no formal credit given for the course. We will mostly communicate by email, so if you're interested in the course and not already getting emails about it, reach out to either of us to make sure you're on our mailing list.

### Course Outline:

A tentative outline of the course is:

  1. The Super-Poincaré Algebra
  2. Superspace and Superfields
  3. Chiral Superfields and Supersymmetric Lagrangians
  4. Spontaneous Breaking of Supersymmetry
  5. Supersymmetric Gauge Theories
  6. The Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model
  7. Supersymmetric Phenomenology
  8. Exact Results in Supersymmetric Gauge Theories


### Textbook & Resources

The main text for the first half of the class will be _[Introduction to Supersymmetry](https://doi.org/10.1142/7594)_ by Harald Müller-Kirsten and Armin Wiedemann. This book covers only the basic structure of global supersymmetry, but goes through all the relevant calculations in explicit detail.

There are a number of other useful references for supersymmetry. Some you might find particularly useful are:
- David Tong's Notes on _[Supersymmetric Field Theory](https://davidtong.org/teaching/supersymmetric-field-theory/)_
- _[A Supersymmetry Primer](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9709356)_ by Steve Martin
- _[From Spinors to Supersymmetry](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781139049740)_ by Dreiner, Haber and Martin
- _[Supergravity](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139026833)_ by Freedman and Van Proeyen
- _[Theory and Phenomenology of Sparticles](https://doi.org/10.1142/4001)_ by Drees, Godbole and Roy

The second half of the course will deal with more specialized applications, both to phenomenology and formal theory, which will draw heavily from these (and other) references.
